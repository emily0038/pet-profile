Building your pet sitting business is all about making connections. Facebook groups are an accessible way to connect with industry peers and potential clients, enabling you to make personal connections without meeting up IRL.

Whether you're a seasoned pet sitter or just starting out, here are some groups to check out to get started.

## Top Pet Sitter Networking Groups

Getting advice from other professionals can help you level up your pet sitting business. In an industry with many hobbyists and part-timers, connecting with full-time caregivers and experienced business owners provides valuable insights you won't find elsewhere.

National and global groups often feature more in-depth conversations about business strategy, industry trends, and professional challenges since they draw from a larger, more diverse membership base.

Many professionals lean on these communities for practical advice on everything from pricing and contracts to handling difficult situations. These groups can also help you connect with pet sitters in other markets, which may lead to referral partnerships when your clients travel or relocate.

However, like any online community, Facebook groups vary widely in quality. As a general rule, public groups tend to have more spam and less substantive discussion. Since there are no barriers to entry, they often attract more casual or beginner sitters and may be less strictly moderated. Private groups that require proof of credentials or business licensing typically offer more focused, professional conversations.

Here are some of the top groups for pet sitting professional development and what to expect when you join.

### Dog Walker to Dog Walker — international

- Private  
- 18.6k members

[Dog Walker to Dog Walker - international](https://www.facebook.com/groups/1437387859824215) was created for dog walkers and pet care providers to discuss relevant topics, regardless of geography. Members engage in a range of conversations, with everything from venting about on-the-job experiences to seeking professional advice.

Because it’s an international group, questions are typically kept more general, or the poster may include region-based caveats. You’ll see posts requesting waterproof seat cover recommendations, polling about cancellation policies, and even asking advice about a situation with a customer.

Engagement rates tend to be pretty high, oftentimes with thorough and supportive responses.

**Rules:**  
Members are asked to prioritize privacy by keeping all discussions within the group, and by anonymizing stories when necessary.

Admins also restrict certain activity, such as:

1. Soliciting clients, jobs, employees, or vendors  
2. Hate speech, bullying, or abusive posts  
3. Side hustles, promotions, or spam  
4. Social causes or politics

**Who can join:**  
Despite the name, this group isn’t just for dog walkers. In addition to independent pet care providers and business owners, it’s also open to more casual enthusiasts, including pet bloggers or those interested in starting a pet care business.

### PET SITTERS FORUM

- Private  
- 2.4k members

[PET SITTERS FORUM](https://www.facebook.com/groups/Petsittersforum) is made for pet sitters looking for a community to advise, support, and challenge them in their professional journey. Since it’s a bit of a smaller group, there’s not as much regular activity, but it’s designed to attract quality over quantity.

**Rules:**  
While admins invite healthy disagreements and differing opinions, they make it clear that they won’t tolerate personal attacks. They also don’t allow selling, distribution, spam, or donation requests.

**Who can join:**  
This group is exclusively for professional pet sitters whose pet care business is their sole or primary source of income.

In order to join, you must have a pet sitting company listed as your business or employment on your Facebook profile. You also must provide your [business website](https://www.petsfriendz.com/blog/how-to-grow-your-business-with-your-pet-sitting-website) or profile, as well as your pet sitting insurance carrier, when you submit your request.

### Pet Sitter Support Group

- Private  
- 2.5k members

Similar to Pet Sitters Forum, [Pet Sitter Support Group](https://www.facebook.com/groups/343088152548500) is designed to be a community for pet sitters to lean on each other for advice and support.

Members help each other navigate pet care-related questions, situations, and dilemmas. Admins ensure that all activity is kept respectful and comments are supportive.

**Rules:**  
Since this group is meant to be a safe space for pet sitters, members are advised not to share anything outside the group. They’re also warned against inviting any clients.

Admins also restrict activity including:

1. Hate speech or bullying  
2. Profanity  
3. Self-promotion, memes, or spam  
4. TikToks or Reels

**Who can join:**  
This group is only for professional pet care providers with an established business. When requesting to join the group, you’ll need to provide a link to your business website or profile, with confirmation that you’ve been in business for the minimum of one year.

## The Benefits of Joining Professional Pet Sitter Facebook Groups

Before joining a new community, you’ll want to have an idea of what you’re looking to get out of it. Facebook groups offer pet sitters unique opportunities that other marketing channels simply can't match, from networking to guidance from other professionals.

### Accelerate Your Professional Development

Starting your own pet sitting business comes with a lot of responsibilities, including many that have nothing to do with caring for pets. Even when running a solo venture, you’re in charge of everything from service contracts to insurance.

Joining a pet sitting Facebook group gives you access to a network of seasoned professionals who can offer valuable advice on the business side. Especially in private groups, many members have been in the industry for years. You’ll be able to easily search the group for topics like best practices, new pet care techniques, useful software, and pricing strategies.

### Lean on Other Sitters When You Need Support

Being an independent pet sitter can feel isolating, especially when you’re just starting out. Professional pet sitter groups provide a supportive community where you can connect with others who understand the unique challenges of your work, from handling last-minute cancellations to managing difficult client situations.

With so many pet care providers facing burnout, these spaces can offer much-needed help in difficult times, making the job more manageable. Leaning on your community can also provide professional benefits, as seeking guidance can prevent you from acting out emotionally in stressful situations or making [beginner mistakes](https://www.petsfriendz.com/blog/myths-hurting-your-pet-sitting-business).

### Stay Plugged Into the Industry

If you want to proactively improve your business, it’s important to be informed about what’s happening in the industry. Facebook groups are a great online outlet to help you stay on top of timely topics like upcoming legislation, new pet care research, and evolving best practices.

This knowledge can translate to real business advantages. It can help you spot emerging trends in pet care, learn what competitors in your area are doing, and identify gaps in the market that your business could fill. By staying plugged in, you can also ensure you're always providing current, high-quality care that meets or exceeds industry standards.

## More Resources to Advance Your Pet Care Business

Facebook groups aren’t the only way to connect with the pet sitting community. Other channels like newsletters, influencers, and social media accounts can also help you stay plugged into the latest in the pet care world.
